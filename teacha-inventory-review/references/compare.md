# Compare inventory evidence

1. Open the intended branch and completed inventory record. Capture its timestamp, item identity and displayed unit; do not mistake an editable draft or current-stock dashboard for the historical record.
2. Identify which expected/opening quantities the application actually stores. Read current documentation or source when calculation semantics are unclear. Do not substitute the previous physical count for expected stock.
3. Inspect receipts, transfers in/out, adjustments, returns, reversals and recorded consumption between comparable observation times. Avoid double counting reversed movements. Match branch, item and units before arithmetic.
4. For a comparable physical reconciliation, difference is counted minus expected: negative means shortage relative to expectation, positive means surplus. Label any alternative display convention explicitly. Null/missing means unknown, not zero; retain fractional units.
5. When comparing two counts, account for intervening movements and interval coverage. An incomplete count or limited item selection cannot establish whole-store consumption. A recipe explains ingredient amounts per preparation, not how many preparations occurred.
6. Use only source-supported unit/package conversions. If density, package size, yield or a recipe amount is missing, leave the converted estimate unresolved.
7. Present evidence and uncertainty before recommendations. Check relevant approved recipes through their browse-only interface; editing a recipe is a separate request. Do not save forms, classify discrepancies or backfill movements while reviewing.
